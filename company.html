Company

Built by the people who had to make the numbers defensible.

VissRad came out of industrial estimating and project controls work — piping and equipment takeoff, P6 schedule data, earned-value reporting, procurement and payables — not out of a product roadmap. Every screen exists because a real project needed it and the spreadsheet had run out of road.

The problem was never the arithmetic.

On industrial capital projects the estimate, the schedule, the field, the cost report and the payables desk each keep a version of the truth. They are all defensible on their own. Put together, they disagree — and by the time anyone notices, the invoice is paid.

Every commercial tool available either solves one slice of that beautifully and ignores the rest, or solves all of it at a price and a rollout length that only suits an owner-operator with a programme office. Neither shape helps an EPC team that needs the chain to hold next month.

So VissRad was built the other way round: small applications a discipline can actually adopt, exchanging documents in the same shape the business already uses, with the reporting exported to wherever the organisation already reads its numbers.

How we build

Three commitments we hold ourselves to.

Evidence before status

A percentage is not progress. A gate does not open because the schedule needs it to. Every status in VissRad has a quantity, a signed check sheet or a certificate behind it, and the software will show you which.

Own your data

A project is a file you can copy. The reporting layer exports star schemas and speaks ODBC. Nothing is locked behind a hosted tenancy, and moving off VissRad is a supported operation rather than a negotiation.

Say what is not built

Stubs are labelled as stubs. Demo payment files are stamped. The P6 exchange is described as a file import because that is what it is. Overselling a project controls tool costs the buyer far more than it costs us.
